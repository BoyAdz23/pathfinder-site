01 / The experience

Not a quiz. A situation that moves.

Each situation is a short challenge in six stages, built around one workplace in the middle of something: a picture, a brief, and people who don't report to the candidate. Partway through, the picture changes. Every stage is a small challenge of its own, and every one feeds the profile.

Pick out what would genuinely change their mind, and leave the red herrings.

A lucky guess earns nothing.

In a situational judgement test, picking the right option is enough. In Turnstone a decision only counts when the candidate's own findings back it.

03 / The baseline profile

One baseline profile. The same for every candidate.

Every candidate sits the same four situations and three reasoning tests, scored by the same rules. What comes out is a baseline profile: a picture of how they lead that you can put next to anyone else's.

What you get

The seven readings, a plain-English paragraph on each competency, the six-line radar and the overall reading, with suggested questions drawn from what the candidate did.

Why the same matters

Interviews differ from candidate to candidate. The baseline doesn't. Compare two or three candidates side by side, read a whole shortlist in one table, and come back to the same evidence next time you hire.

What it is for

Evidence beside your interview notes. It helps you show a client or a hiring panel why these candidates are on the shortlist. Sort the table by any reading you like; there is no pass mark, so the decision stays with you.

05 / The problem

Rank is not a competency measure.

How a service leaver gets things done in a civilian workplace, where rank no longer gives the order, is the thing a CV and an interview find hardest to show.

CVs and interviews do not translate military competence

Operational judgement, command decision-making and analytical rigour are compressed into job titles and dates. The substance is lost.

Employers use rank as a proxy for capability

Two officers of identical rank can differ substantially in how they work. Rank does not distinguish them.

The cost is borne on both sides

Mismatched hires, wasted capability, and capable people passed over because their experience doesn't read across.

06 / The hardest part of the move

In the forces, authority comes with the role. In a civilian organisation, it often doesn't.

A new senior hire from the forces usually has to get things done through people who don't report to them: a founder, investors, a partner, a team they have only just met. Turnstone is built around exactly that. In every situation the candidate arrives new, in a civilian workplace, with no one to give orders to. You can't order; you have to ask.

The CV

What they have done, in their own words, often in language a civilian employer can't read.

Reasoning tests

How they handle numbers, words and logic.

The interview

How they present, and what they choose to tell you.

Turnstone

What they actually do with a situation they didn't create, people they don't control, and news that changes the picture.

07 / How it is scored

No AI marks anyone.

Some assessment tools now use AI to judge a candidate's answers, so the result rests on a system that cannot fully explain itself or be relied on to give the same answer twice. Turnstone takes AI out of marking altogether.

Deterministic

The same choices, the same result

Scoring is code, run on the server. Identical choices yield an identical result on every run, for every candidate.

Structured

Judgement has to be backed

A decision earns credit only when the candidate's own board supports it. Picking the right option without the reasoning behind it earns nothing.

Traceable

Nothing a black box decided

Every line of the profile traces back to something the candidate did. Defensible to a hiring panel, or to a candidate who asks why.

A reading, never a verdict

Each finished profile carries an overall reading (Strong, Solid, Developing or Limited) built from the four situations alone. The candidates table opens by it, and can be sorted by any competency or test. There is no pass mark and no automatic rejection: the decision stays with you.
